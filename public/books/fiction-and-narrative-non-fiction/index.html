Il senso della natura. Sette sentieri per la terra

Looking an orangutan, an octopus or a whale shark in the eye. Setting out to understand a desert, a forest or a mountain range by way of the traditions of human thought. Travelling from New York to the Galápagos, from Iceland to Borneo, from Rwanda to Tibet, to immerse ourselves in our home, the planet we are called to look after. A planet whose future has yet to be written: in recent years we have come to recognise the destructive impact of human civilisation on nature, made evident by climate catastrophes, the extinction of entire animal species, desertification and the disappearance of landscapes. The discovery of a way of caring for the world and a new definition of ecology, that “magnificent science that has become sad”, call for a vision of the future that imagines other ways of perceiving nature, and for a recovery of our biological memory that lets us feel the indissoluble, living, organic and inorganic unity of everything that exists on Earth.

Il dio che danza

Il dio che danza (The Dancing God) recounts the author's journeys in search of an ancient and universal phenomenon: the possession trance induced by dance and music. In ancient Greece it was practised in the name of Dionysus, Homer's “mad god”. But Dionysus was also the “giver of joy”: the god “who loosens”, “who sets free”, letting life break through the fragile boundaries of the self and of social norms.
